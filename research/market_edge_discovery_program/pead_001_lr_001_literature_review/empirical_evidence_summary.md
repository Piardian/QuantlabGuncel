# Empirical Evidence Summary

Strongly supported:

- PEAD is a major documented anomaly in accounting and finance.
- Drift direction is related to earnings surprise direction.
- PEAD has been studied across several decades and methodological variants.

Moderately supported:

- Analyst-forecast-based surprises can produce stronger drift than time-series surprises.
- Liquidity and transaction costs materially affect realized implementation.

Conflicting evidence:

- Some modern studies question broad persistence at firm-level disaggregation.
- Post-publication decay and crowding can reduce anomaly returns.
- Results are sensitive to data timing, surprise definition and cost assumptions.

Unsupported at LR stage:

- PEAD profitability in this project.
- PEAD robustness in the available local data.
- Any production implementation.

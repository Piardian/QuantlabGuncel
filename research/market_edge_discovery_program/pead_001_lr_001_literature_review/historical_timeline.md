# Historical Timeline

1968:

Ball and Brown document the association between accounting earnings and security returns and observe continued abnormal return movement after earnings news.

1977:

Foster studies quarterly accounting data and predictive properties of earnings.

1984:

Foster, Olsen and Shevlin document earnings-release anomalies and systematic security-return behavior after announcements.

1989:

Bernard and Thomas publish the canonical PEAD study framing the anomaly as delayed price response or risk premium.

1990:

Bernard and Thomas provide further evidence that prices do not fully reflect implications of current earnings for future earnings.

2000s:

Research expands into analyst forecasts, revenue surprises, liquidity, transaction costs and institutional explanations.

2010s-2020s:

Modern literature examines publication decay, implementation frictions, international evidence, order flow, and whether PEAD remains robust after stricter data and trading-cost assumptions.

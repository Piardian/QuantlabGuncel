# Earnings Surprise Measurement

The literature uses several surprise definitions.

## Time-Series SUE

Standardized Unexpected Earnings based on historical seasonal earnings patterns.

Advantages:

- Can be constructed from accounting data.
- Does not require analyst forecasts.

Risks:

- Accounting data restatements can create point-in-time issues.
- Seasonal model assumptions may be weak for changing firms.

## Analyst Forecast Surprise

Actual EPS minus analyst consensus estimate, usually standardized by price or forecast dispersion.

Advantages:

- Often closer to market expectations.
- Livnat and Mendenhall report stronger drift using analyst-based surprises than time-series measures.

Risks:

- Requires point-in-time consensus snapshots.
- Revised or stale estimates can leak future information.

## Announcement-Window Return Surprise

Uses immediate price reaction around the announcement as a market-based surprise proxy.

Advantages:

- Directly observes market reaction.

Risks:

- Can blend surprise measurement with price-impact measurement.
- Requires careful timing, especially after-close and before-open events.

## CD-001 Implication

PEAD-001 cannot safely define surprise without choosing a point-in-time-safe data source and event-timing policy.

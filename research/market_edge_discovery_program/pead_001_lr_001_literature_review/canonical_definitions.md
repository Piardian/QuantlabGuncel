# Canonical Definitions

PEAD is usually defined as:

The tendency for stock prices to continue moving in the direction of an earnings surprise after the earnings announcement has become public.

Canonical empirical structure:

- Identify quarterly earnings announcements.
- Measure unexpected earnings or earnings surprise.
- Sort firms by surprise magnitude.
- Measure post-announcement abnormal returns.
- Compare positive-surprise and negative-surprise portfolios.

Important distinction:

PEAD is not simply price momentum. It is event-conditioned drift after earnings news.

The construct depends on the announcement event and surprise measurement.

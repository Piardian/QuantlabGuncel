# Historical Timeline

## Pre-Academic Trend Following

Trend-following ideas predate modern empirical finance and appear in practitioner trading traditions long before formal factor research.

## 1990s

Behavioural theories of underreaction, overreaction and gradual information diffusion provide conceptual foundations relevant to trend persistence.

## 2012

Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen publish *Time Series Momentum* in the Journal of Financial Economics. This becomes the canonical modern academic reference for TSM.

## 2013

Asness, Moskowitz and Pedersen document value and momentum across asset classes, reinforcing broad factor relevance while keeping cross-sectional and time-series constructs conceptually distinct.

## 2016

Kim, Tse and Wald challenge the interpretation of TSM profits by arguing that volatility scaling explains a substantial portion of reported performance.

## 2017

Hurst, Ooi and Pedersen publish long-history trend-following evidence extending analysis back to 1880 across multiple markets.

## 2020s

Replication, robustness and implementation debates continue, especially around volatility scaling, trading costs, crisis behavior and asset-class scope.

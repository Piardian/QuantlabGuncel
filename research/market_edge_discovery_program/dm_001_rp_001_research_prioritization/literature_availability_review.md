# Literature Availability Review

## Assessment

**Moderate direct literature availability; high component-literature availability.**

## Foundational Component Literature

- [Jegadeesh and Titman (1993)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1993.tb04702.x): foundational cross-sectional momentum.
- [Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2011.11.003): canonical time-series momentum and comparison with cross-sectional momentum.
- [Asness, Moskowitz and Pedersen (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12021): momentum across markets and asset classes.

## Direct Dual Momentum Literature

- [Antonacci, Risk Premia Harvesting Through Dual Momentum](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2042750): principal direct formulation combining relative and absolute momentum; practitioner-origin evidence.
- [Lim, Wang and Yao (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbankfin.2018.10.010): peer-reviewed stock-level design combining own-return sign with cross-sectional sorts.
- [Ha and Fabozzi (2022)](https://doi.org/10.3905/jpm.2022.1.336): direct portfolio-oriented Dual Momentum study.

## Replication and Skepticism Context

- [Harvey, Liu and Zhu (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhv059): multiple-testing discipline.
- [McLean and Pontiff (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12365): out-of-sample and post-publication decay.
- [Open Source Asset Pricing](https://www.openassetpricing.com/): reproducible cross-sectional predictor resources.
- [Kim, Tse and Wald (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.finmar.2016.05.003): evidence that volatility scaling can materially affect time-series momentum conclusions.

## Availability Conclusion

The literature is sufficient to study Dual Momentum as a combination or interaction. It is not comparably mature in establishing a unique Dual Momentum mechanism independent of the cross-sectional and time-series components.

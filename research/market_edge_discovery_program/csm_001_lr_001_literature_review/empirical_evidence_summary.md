# Empirical Evidence Summary

## U.S. Equities

**Evidence status: Strong historical support.**

The foundational U.S. equity literature documents intermediate-horizon continuation among past winners relative to past losers. Evidence is strongest for carefully constructed cross-sectional tests and factor portfolios.

## International Equities

**Evidence status: Strong but qualified.**

International evidence generally supports momentum, but strength varies by region and period. Japan is often noted as a weaker or more inconsistent case in global studies.

## Cross-Asset Evidence

**Evidence status: Moderate to strong but not identical.**

Momentum-like behavior appears across asset classes, but cross-asset and time-series momentum are related constructs, not direct substitutes for equity cross-sectional momentum.

## Mutual Fund and Factor Model Evidence

**Evidence status: Strong as explanatory factor.**

Carhart's four-factor model established momentum as an important control in fund-performance attribution.

## Replication Evidence

**Evidence status: Qualified.**

Large-scale replication work supports caution: many anomalies weaken under stricter testing, but momentum remains one of the more important and persistent families.

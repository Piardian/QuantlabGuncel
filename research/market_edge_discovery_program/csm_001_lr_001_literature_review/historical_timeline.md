# Historical Timeline

## 1993

Jegadeesh and Titman document that buying past winners and selling past losers generates positive intermediate-horizon returns in U.S. equities.

## 1997

Carhart adds a momentum factor to mutual fund performance analysis, embedding momentum into standard empirical asset-pricing practice.

## Late 1990s

Behavioral models such as Daniel, Hirshleifer and Subrahmanyam and Hong and Stein provide theoretical explanations based on biased beliefs, underreaction and information diffusion.

## 2000s

Momentum becomes a central anomaly in factor investing, mutual fund evaluation and empirical asset-pricing tests.

## 2012

Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen broaden attention to time-series momentum across futures markets. This is related but distinct from cross-sectional momentum.

## 2013

Asness, Moskowitz and Pedersen document value and momentum patterns across asset classes, emphasizing common structure and interaction with value.

## 2016

Harvey, Liu and Zhu sharpen the factor-zoo and multiple-testing critique. McLean and Pontiff document out-of-sample and post-publication decay across published predictors.

## 2020s

Open Asset Pricing and large-scale replication programs provide broader reproducibility infrastructure, while debate continues about robustness, implementation costs and anomaly decay.

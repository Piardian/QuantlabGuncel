# Behavioural vs Risk-Based Explanations

## Behavioural Explanations

Behavioural models generally explain momentum through slow price adjustment, investor underreaction, overconfidence, biased self-attribution, attention limits or gradual information diffusion.

### Evidence Status

**Moderately to strongly supported as plausible theory.**

These models explain why intermediate-horizon continuation can coexist with longer-horizon reversal. However, behavioural explanations are not uniquely identified by the empirical evidence.

## Risk-Based Explanations

Risk-based explanations argue that momentum earns compensation for exposure to systematic risk, crash risk, liquidity risk, funding risk or macroeconomic states.

### Evidence Status

**Moderate and conflicting.**

Momentum crashes and state dependence support the view that momentum is not a free lunch. But no single risk model fully absorbs the anomaly across all settings.

## Institutional and Market Friction Explanations

Institutional explanations emphasize delegated management, benchmark constraints, slow-moving capital, limits to arbitrage and investor flows.

### Evidence Status

**Moderate.**

These explanations are compatible with momentum persistence and crowding, but operational measurement is difficult.

## LR-001 Conclusion

The literature does not settle the mechanism. Future stages should distinguish construct validity, mechanism validation, predictive validation and economic validation.

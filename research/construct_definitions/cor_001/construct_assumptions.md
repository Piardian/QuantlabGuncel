# Construct Assumptions

## Primary Assumption

Average pairwise realized correlation is a valid observable proxy for broad US equity market co-movement.

Evidence classification: Supported by literature as a measurement family.

## Assumption 1: Daily Log Returns Are Appropriate Inputs

The construct assumes daily log returns provide a reproducible basis for measuring realized co-movement.

Evidence classification: Supported by standard financial econometrics practice.

## Assumption 2: A 60-Day Window Captures Current Correlation State

The construct assumes 60 trading days is long enough to estimate a broad correlation state while remaining responsive to market changes.

Evidence classification: Plausible but not predictive.

## Assumption 3: Pearson Correlation Is Sufficient For First Construct Version

The construct assumes linear realized co-movement is the correct first operationalization.

Evidence classification: Supported by simplicity and literature maturity, with known limitations.

## Assumption 4: A Fixed Universe Supports Reproducibility

The construct assumes a fixed research universe is appropriate for deterministic research infrastructure.

Evidence classification: Supported for reproducibility, limited by survivorship concerns.

## Assumption 5: Average Off-Diagonal Correlation Represents Market Synchronization

The construct assumes the mean of pairwise correlations summarizes broad cross-sectional co-movement.

Evidence classification: Supported as a descriptive correlation-state proxy.

## Assumption 6: 252-Day Normalization Provides Relative State Context

The construct assumes one trading year is a reasonable historical context for z-score and percentile normalization.

Evidence classification: Operationally standard, not a predictive claim.

## Non-Assumptions

COR-001 does not assume high correlation predicts negative returns.

COR-001 does not assume low correlation predicts positive returns.

COR-001 does not assume economic utility.

COR-001 does not assume causality.

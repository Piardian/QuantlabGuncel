# Executive Summary

The literature strongly supports the idea that market regimes exist as persistent states in financial data.
The most established regime definitions are bull/bear, high/low volatility, risk-on/risk-off, and latent hidden-state regimes.

The strongest academic foundation comes from Markov-switching and hidden-state econometrics, beginning with Hamilton (1989) and extended by later finance surveys and asset-allocation papers.

Observable variables most often used to identify regimes are price returns, volatility, correlations, breadth, and macro or stress proxies.

The literature is less settled on one universal regime taxonomy or one best operational definition.
That lack of consensus is a real research gap, not a failure of the field.

This review does not make any claim about predictive validity, economic value, or production suitability.
Its purpose is to establish the scientific basis for CD-001.

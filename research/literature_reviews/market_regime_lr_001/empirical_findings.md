# Empirical Findings

## Strongly Supported

- Financial markets exhibit regime-like behavior rather than a single stable linear process. [Hamilton 1989](https://www.ssc.wisc.edu/~bhansen/718/Hamilton1989.pdf), [Ang & Timmermann 2012](https://ideas.repec.org/a/anr/refeco/v4y2012p313-337.html)
- Bull and bear phases are widely observed and heavily studied.
- Volatility regimes are particularly well documented and often align with stress periods. [ECB volatility regime note](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/financial-stability-publications/fsr/focus/2018/pdf/ecb~bcaaae16c3.fsrbox201805_03.pdf)

## Moderately Supported

- Regime-aware portfolio construction can improve out-of-sample behavior in some settings, especially when the regime model is used for allocation or risk control. [Ang & Bekaert 2003](https://ideas.repec.org/p/nbr/nberwo/10080.html), [Asset allocation under multivariate regime switching](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/wp/2005/2005-002.pdf)
- Regime classifications can differ across asset classes and across time. [Market regime classification using correlation networks](https://econ.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/1423/2019-Mayo-Zhu_Nam.pdf), [Incorporating Market Regimes into Large-Scale Stock Portfolios](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/121552/1/MPRA_paper_121552.pdf)

## Limited Evidence

- Breadth, sector leadership, and liquidity are sometimes used to define or interpret regimes, but the literature is less standardized here than for returns and volatility.
- Machine-learning regime classifiers are promising, but the evidence base is newer and more heterogeneous than the classic Markov-switching literature.

## Conflicting Evidence

- Whether regime detection is best done in real time or ex post.
- Whether a small number of universal states is adequate for equity markets.
- Whether regime labels should be interpreted as economic states or merely as statistically useful partitions.

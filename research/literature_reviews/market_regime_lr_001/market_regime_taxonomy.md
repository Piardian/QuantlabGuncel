# Market Regime Taxonomy

This taxonomy is a descriptive synthesis of how the literature commonly groups market regimes.

## Strongly Supported Categories

### Bull / Bear
- Bull markets: sustained positive returns, typically lower volatility.
- Bear markets: sustained negative returns, often higher volatility and drawdowns.

### High Volatility / Low Volatility
- A very common financial regime split, especially in return and risk modeling.

### Regime-Switching States
- Latent states inferred from Markov-switching or HMM models.
- Often interpreted after estimation as bull, bear, stress, or tranquil regimes.

## Moderately Supported Categories

### Expansion / Contraction
- Common in macro-finance and financial cycle work.
- Often aligned with volatility, liquidity, and breadth changes.

### Risk-On / Risk-Off
- Widely used by practitioners.
- Usually a composite interpretation of return, volatility, credit, and correlation behavior.

## Limited-Evidence Categories

### Breadth Regimes
- Market breadth or participation regimes are discussed, but less standardized than bull/bear or volatility regimes.

### Liquidity Regimes
- Relevant in microstructure and stress settings, but less consistently operationalized in broad equity regime papers.

### Sector-Leadership Regimes
- Appears in practitioner research and portfolio construction work, but lacks a single canonical academic taxonomy.

## Conflicting / Unsettled Areas

- Whether regimes should be defined by price only, or by price plus macro and cross-asset state variables.
- Whether regimes are best modeled as discrete states or continuous latent factors.
- Whether ex post labels like bull/bear are preferable to statistically inferred latent states.

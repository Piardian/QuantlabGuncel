# Theoretical Foundations

## Transaction Cost Theory

Liquidity reflects the cost of converting an asset to cash or changing a position. Bid-ask spreads and effective spreads are direct expressions of trading costs.

## Inventory Risk

Market makers and liquidity providers bear inventory risk. When inventory risk is high, liquidity provision becomes more expensive and spreads widen.

## Asymmetric Information

If liquidity providers suspect informed trading, they demand compensation through wider spreads or greater price impact.

## Price Impact

Large or informed trades move prices. Kyle-style models formalize this through market depth and price impact.

## Funding Liquidity Interaction

Funding liquidity and market liquidity can reinforce each other. When traders face tighter funding constraints, they provide less market liquidity, which can amplify market stress.

## Commonality in Liquidity

Liquidity can move together across securities. This supports studying liquidity as both an asset-level and market-wide construct.

## Scientific Interpretation

The literature supports liquidity as a market-quality and trading-friction construct, not merely as a synonym for volume or volatility.

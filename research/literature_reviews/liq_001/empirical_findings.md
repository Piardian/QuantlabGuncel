# Empirical Findings

## Established Findings

- Liquidity is multidimensional and can be measured through spread, volume, depth, price impact, and resiliency proxies.
- Liquidity varies through time and across assets.
- Market-wide liquidity can behave as a systematic state variable.
- Liquidity tends to deteriorate during stress periods.
- Funding constraints can reinforce poor market liquidity.
- Illiquid securities often exhibit different expected-return behavior than liquid securities in asset-pricing studies.

## Asset Pricing Evidence

Amihud-style illiquidity, Pastor-Stambaugh liquidity risk, and Acharya-Pedersen liquidity-adjusted asset pricing are major strands of the empirical literature.

These studies support liquidity as economically meaningful in broad finance literature.

LR-001 does not test whether these findings hold in this project.

## Market Microstructure Evidence

Microstructure research supports liquidity as a core market-quality variable. Spread, depth, price impact, and resiliency are repeatedly used to understand trading cost and price formation.

## Stress and Stability Evidence

Policy and central-bank literature treat liquidity as relevant to financial stability. Liquidity deterioration is commonly associated with stress environments and market fragility.

## Important Boundary

The literature shows that liquidity is important. It does not automatically imply that any specific LIQ-001 implementation will be predictive, profitable, or economically useful.

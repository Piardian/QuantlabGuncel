# Empirical Findings

## Purpose

Summarize empirical findings reported in the literature. This file does not independently test predictive validity.

## Finding 1: Volatility Is Time-Varying

Financial return volatility changes over time rather than remaining constant.

Evidence category:

**Strongly supported by literature**

## Finding 2: Volatility Clusters

Large absolute price changes tend to cluster, and low-volatility periods tend to cluster.

Evidence category:

**Strongly supported by literature**

## Finding 3: Conditional Volatility Models Are Foundational

ARCH and GARCH models formalize time-varying conditional variance and remain foundational in volatility modeling.

Evidence category:

**Strongly supported by literature**

## Finding 4: Realized Volatility Is a Major Measurement Framework

Realized volatility and realized variance are widely used as ex-post measures of return variation, especially when high-frequency data is available.

Evidence category:

**Strongly supported by literature**

## Finding 5: Range-Based Estimators Add Information From OHLC Data

Range-based estimators exploit high, low, open and close prices to measure volatility more efficiently than close-only data under their assumptions.

Evidence category:

**Strongly supported by literature**

## Finding 6: Implied Volatility Measures Expected Future Volatility

VIX-style implied volatility indices are designed to measure market expectations of near-term equity-market volatility using option prices.

Evidence category:

**Strongly supported by official methodology and practitioner literature**

## Finding 7: Measurement Choice Matters

Different volatility estimators can represent different economic objects and can behave differently across markets, horizons, jumps and trading sessions.

Evidence category:

**Strongly supported by literature**

## Finding 8: Volatility Is Not Alpha

The literature treats volatility primarily as a risk, uncertainty, pricing and state variable. A volatility construct does not automatically imply directional return prediction or trading profitability.

Evidence category:

**Strongly supported by interpretation of literature scope**

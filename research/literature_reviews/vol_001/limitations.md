# Limitations

## 1. Measurement Plurality

There is no single universal volatility measure. Different measures estimate different aspects of variation.

Evidence category:

**Strongly supported**

## 2. Data Frequency Dependence

Daily, intraday and option data support different volatility definitions. A construct available from daily OHLC data is not equivalent to one based on high-frequency or option data.

Evidence category:

**Strongly supported**

## 3. Estimator Assumptions

Range-based estimators rely on assumptions about price processes, drift, jumps, overnight gaps and continuous trading.

Evidence category:

**Strongly supported**

## 4. Microstructure Noise

High-frequency realized volatility can be affected by bid-ask bounce, discreteness, asynchronous trading and other microstructure effects.

Evidence category:

**Strongly supported**

## 5. Implied Volatility Interpretation

Implied volatility reflects expectations and risk premia, not pure expected realized volatility.

Evidence category:

**Strongly supported**

## 6. Parameter and Window Dependence

Rolling volatility estimates require lookback choices. Conditional volatility models require model and estimation choices.

Evidence category:

**Strongly supported**

## 7. Volatility vs Risk

Volatility is an important risk proxy but does not capture all dimensions of risk, such as skewness, tail loss, liquidity, drawdown path or correlation breakdown.

Evidence category:

**Moderately supported**

## LR-001 Boundary

These limitations do not reject VOL-001. They define constraints CD-001 must address when freezing one narrow construct.

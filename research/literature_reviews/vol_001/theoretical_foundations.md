# Theoretical Foundations

## 1. Volatility as Risk

Volatility is a foundational risk concept because it measures dispersion of possible price or return outcomes.

In portfolio theory and risk management, variance and standard deviation are central measures of uncertainty, even though they are not complete descriptions of downside risk.

Evidence category:

**Strongly supported by literature**

## 2. Volatility Clustering

Financial time series commonly exhibit volatility clustering: large price changes tend to cluster with large changes, and quiet periods tend to cluster with quiet periods.

This observation motivates conditional volatility models such as ARCH and GARCH.

Evidence category:

**Strongly supported by literature**

## 3. Conditional Heteroskedasticity

ARCH and GARCH frameworks model current conditional variance as a function of previous shocks and previous conditional variance.

This provides a formal mechanism for persistent volatility states.

Evidence category:

**Strongly supported by literature**

## 4. Information Arrival

Volatility can rise when new information changes prices rapidly or increases uncertainty about fair value.

This mechanism is broad and not tied to one estimator.

Evidence category:

**Moderately supported**

## 5. Leverage and Asymmetry

Equity markets often show asymmetric volatility behavior: negative shocks are frequently associated with larger subsequent volatility increases than positive shocks.

Evidence category:

**Moderately supported**

## 6. Implied Expectations

Option prices embed market expectations and risk premia about future volatility. VIX-style measures operationalize this idea for equity index options.

Evidence category:

**Strongly supported by practitioner methodology and options literature**

## 7. Market Stress

High volatility often coincides with stress, deleveraging, funding pressure, liquidity deterioration and uncertainty.

Evidence category:

**Moderately supported**

## Boundary

These theoretical foundations do not imply trading profitability or alpha.

They only justify volatility as a meaningful scientific construct for further definition and validation.

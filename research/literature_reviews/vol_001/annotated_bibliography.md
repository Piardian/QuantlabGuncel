# Annotated Bibliography

## Engle (1982), Autoregressive Conditional Heteroscedasticity

- **Link:** https://www.jstor.org/stable/1912773
- **Use:** Foundational ARCH model.
- **Takeaway:** Provides a formal framework for time-varying conditional variance.

## Bollerslev (1986), Generalized Autoregressive Conditional Heteroskedasticity

- **Link:** https://public.econ.duke.edu/~boller/Published_Papers/joe_86.pdf
- **Use:** Foundational GARCH model.
- **Takeaway:** Extends ARCH to allow a more flexible lag structure for conditional variance.

## Andersen, Bollerslev, Diebold and Labys (2003), Modeling and Forecasting Realized Volatility

- **Link:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/1468-0262.00418
- **Use:** Foundational realized-volatility framework.
- **Takeaway:** Establishes realized volatility as a major ex-post measurement framework using high-frequency data.

## Andersen, Bollerslev, Diebold and Labys, NBER Working Paper Version

- **Link:** https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w8160/w8160.pdf
- **Use:** Accessible working-paper source for realized-volatility methodology.
- **Takeaway:** Discusses integration of intraday data into volatility measurement and forecasting.

## Parkinson (1980), The Extreme Value Method

- **Link:** https://www-2.rotman.utoronto.ca/~kan/3032/pdf/FinancialAssetReturns/Parkinson_JB_1980.pdf
- **Use:** Foundational high-low range volatility estimator.
- **Takeaway:** Uses extreme high-low values to estimate return variance.

## Garman and Klass (1980), On the Estimation of Security Price Volatilities from Historical Data

- **Link:** https://www-2.rotman.utoronto.ca/~kan/3032/pdf/FinancialAssetReturns/Garman_Klass_JB_1980.pdf
- **Use:** Foundational OHLC volatility estimator.
- **Takeaway:** Uses open, high, low and close information for volatility estimation.

## Rogers and Satchell (1991), Estimating Variance From High, Low and Closing Prices

- **Link:** https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-applied-probability/volume-1/issue-4/Estimating-Variance-From-High-Low-and-Closing-Prices/10.1214/aoap/1177005835.short
- **Use:** Range-based estimator robust to drift assumptions.
- **Takeaway:** Proposes an estimator using high, low and close-related price information.

## Yang and Zhang (2000), Drift-Independent Volatility Estimation

- **Link:** https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.1086/209650
- **Use:** OHLC estimator accounting for drift and opening jumps.
- **Takeaway:** Presents a volatility estimator using multiple periods of high, low, open and close prices.

## Cboe VIX Methodology

- **Link:** https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/indices/Volatility_Index_Methodology_Cboe_Volatility_Index.pdf
- **Use:** Official implied-volatility methodology.
- **Takeaway:** VIX is designed to measure 30-day expected volatility of the US equity market from S&P 500 option prices.

## Cboe VIX Product Overview

- **Link:** https://www.cboe.com/tradable-products/vix/
- **Use:** Practitioner-facing official source.
- **Takeaway:** Describes VIX as a leading measure of near-term volatility expectations conveyed by SPX option prices.

## Cont (2007), Volatility Clustering in Financial Markets

- **Link:** https://rama.cont.perso.math.cnrs.fr/pdf/clustering.pdf
- **Use:** Review of volatility clustering and mechanisms.
- **Takeaway:** Summarizes empirical facts and proposed explanations for clustered volatility.

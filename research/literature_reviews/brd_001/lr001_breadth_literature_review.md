# BRD-001 / LR-001: Market Breadth Literature Review

## Purpose

This literature review evaluates Market Breadth as a financial construct for the Market Signal Discovery Program v3.0.

The review is descriptive. It does not define the final BRD-001 construct, evaluate predictive validity, measure profitability, or recommend a trading application.

## Main Finding

Market Breadth is supported by academic and practitioner literature as a distinct market-internals construct measuring the degree of participation across securities.

The literature support is **moderate to strong**.

The practitioner tradition is mature and extensive. Academic evidence exists, but definitions are less standardized than for volatility, liquidity, or canonical momentum.

## What Market Breadth Represents

Market Breadth asks whether market movement is broadly shared across many securities or concentrated in a smaller subset.

It is a participation construct.

It is not identical to:

- Market direction
- Market volatility
- Market liquidity
- Momentum
- Cross-sectional dispersion
- Index return

Breadth can diverge from index-level price movement because capitalization-weighted indices may rise or fall while the average constituent behaves differently.

## Common Taxonomies

The literature and practitioner research commonly describe breadth through several measurement families:

- Advance / decline participation
- Up-volume / down-volume participation
- New high / new low participation
- Percentage of securities above moving averages
- Breadth momentum and breadth thrust
- Sector or industry breadth
- Equal-weighted versus capitalization-weighted confirmation
- Cross-sectional participation ratios

No single measurement family dominates all use cases.

## Theoretical Foundations

Breadth is usually motivated by internal market confirmation.

The core idea is that broad participation may indicate healthier market movement than narrow leadership, while weak participation may indicate concentration or internal fragility.

Several mechanisms recur in the literature:

- Participation diffusion across securities
- Index concentration and narrow leadership
- Internal confirmation or non-confirmation
- Risk-on / risk-off participation shifts
- Sector-level participation changes
- Investor herding and correlated demand

These mechanisms are plausible, but LR-001 does not test them empirically.

## Measurement Approaches

Breadth can be measured with daily security-panel data using deterministic definitions.

The most common approaches include:

- Number or percentage of advancing securities
- Number or percentage of declining securities
- Cumulative advance / decline lines
- Ratios of advancing to declining issues
- New 52-week highs minus new 52-week lows
- Percentage of securities above 50-day or 200-day moving averages
- Up-volume versus down-volume ratios
- Breadth thrust or breadth momentum indicators
- Sector-level participation counts

Each approach captures a different aspect of participation. CD-001 must freeze one precise operational definition.

## Empirical Evidence

Academic work documents that breadth-related measures can be related to market-wide or cross-sectional behavior, but results depend on definition, universe, horizon, and data construction.

Practitioner literature consistently treats breadth as a useful market-internals lens, especially for diagnosing participation, concentration, internal confirmation, and deterioration beneath index-level performance.

The current evidence is sufficient to justify a dedicated construct-definition stage.

It is not sufficient to claim predictive power, economic value, or production usefulness.

## Key Limitations

Market Breadth is not a single universally standardized scalar.

Major measurement limitations include:

- Dependence on universe definition
- Survivorship bias in historical constituent lists
- Listing and delisting effects
- Exchange composition changes
- Sensitivity to equal-weighted versus capitalization-weighted interpretation
- Sensitivity to lookback window for moving-average and high-low breadth
- Volume-data quality issues for up/down-volume breadth
- Sector composition effects

These limitations must be addressed explicitly in CD-001.

## LR-001 Conclusion

BRD-001 should proceed to `CD-001`.

The literature supports Market Breadth as a standalone construct worthy of formal definition and validation.

The next stage must select one narrow, reproducible breadth definition without using predictive performance, profitability, or optimization as selection criteria.

## Interpretation Boundary

Supported by LR-001:

- Market Breadth is a recognized market-internals construct.
- Breadth measures participation across securities.
- Breadth is conceptually distinct from volatility, liquidity, market regime, momentum, and index return.
- The construct is sufficiently supported to proceed to CD-001.

Not supported by LR-001:

- Any specific breadth indicator is superior.
- Breadth predicts future returns.
- Breadth reduces risk.
- Breadth has economic value.
- Breadth should be used in production.

# BRD-001 / LR-001: Theoretical Foundations

## Purpose

This document summarizes why Market Breadth is considered a meaningful financial construct.

No predictive or economic conclusions are made.

## Participation Theory

Breadth is grounded in the idea that the internal participation of securities matters.

If an index rises because many constituents rise, the market movement is broadly supported.

If an index rises because a small number of high-weight constituents rise, participation is narrow.

Evidence classification:

Moderately supported by literature and strongly supported in practitioner market-internals frameworks.

## Confirmation and Non-Confirmation

Breadth is frequently used as a confirmation lens.

The same index return can occur under different internal conditions:

- Broad confirmation: many securities participate.
- Narrow confirmation: few securities drive the index.
- Divergence: index level improves while participation deteriorates.

Evidence classification:

Strong practitioner support; moderate academic formalization.

## Concentration and Index Weighting

Capitalization-weighted indices can mask weak participation when gains are concentrated in large constituents.

Breadth can therefore represent a cross-sectional participation layer not visible from index returns alone.

Evidence classification:

Strong conceptual support.

## Diffusion of Strength and Weakness

Breadth can be interpreted as measuring how widely strength or weakness has diffused across the market.

This connects breadth to market health and participation persistence, but it is not equivalent to momentum.

Evidence classification:

Moderate support.

## Risk-On / Risk-Off Participation

Broad advances and broad declines may reflect changes in aggregate risk appetite.

Breadth can therefore overlap with market regime, liquidity stress, and volatility state.

However, breadth remains distinct because it measures cross-sectional participation rather than index-level volatility or liquidity.

Evidence classification:

Plausible and moderately supported.

## Sector and Industry Participation

Market participation can broaden or narrow across sectors.

Sector breadth may identify whether market movement is isolated to a narrow leadership group or distributed across the equity market.

Evidence classification:

Moderate support; highly dependent on sector classification.

## Theoretical Foundation Conclusion

The theoretical foundation for Market Breadth is sufficient for a dedicated construct definition.

The strongest theoretical interpretation is:

Market Breadth measures the cross-sectional participation and internal confirmation of market movement.

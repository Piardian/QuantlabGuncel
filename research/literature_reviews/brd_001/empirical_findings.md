# BRD-001 / LR-001: Empirical Findings

## Purpose

This document summarizes reported empirical findings about Market Breadth.

LR-001 does not independently test predictive validity.

## Academic Evidence

Academic literature recognizes breadth-like concepts in several contexts:

- Market-wide participation
- Cross-sectional breadth
- Ownership breadth
- Concentration and dispersion
- Breadth and cross-sectional equity behavior

Some academic studies report relationships between breadth measures and equity returns or cross-sectional performance.

However, definitions vary, and the evidence is less unified than for volatility, liquidity, or canonical momentum.

Evidence classification:

Moderate evidence.

## Practitioner Evidence

Practitioner literature widely uses breadth as a market-internals tool.

Common practitioner applications include:

- Confirming index trends
- Identifying narrow leadership
- Monitoring internal deterioration
- Tracking new highs and new lows
- Measuring percentage of securities above key moving averages
- Detecting participation thrusts

Evidence classification:

Strong practitioner support.

## Areas of Agreement

The literature broadly agrees that breadth measures participation beneath index-level price movement.

There is also broad agreement that breadth can diverge materially from cap-weighted index performance.

## Areas of Disagreement

The literature does not converge on one universal breadth definition.

Disagreement remains around:

- Best measurement family
- Appropriate universe
- Equal-weighted versus cap-weighted interpretation
- Whether breadth is trend, participation, risk, or sentiment
- Whether breadth should be measured daily, smoothed, or thresholded

## Empirical Findings Conclusion

Market Breadth is sufficiently established to proceed to CD-001.

The empirical literature supports breadth as a meaningful observable construct, but not a single canonical implementation.

No LR-001 finding supports predictive, economic, or production claims.

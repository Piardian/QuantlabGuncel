# BRD-001 / LR-001: Market Breadth Taxonomy

## Purpose

This document summarizes the major Market Breadth taxonomies found in academic and practitioner literature.

It does not select the final BRD-001 construct.

## Family A: Advance / Decline Participation

This family measures how many securities rise versus fall over a period.

Common forms:

- Advancing issues
- Declining issues
- Net advances
- Advance / decline ratio
- Cumulative advance / decline line

Construct interpretation:

Market movement is broader when more securities participate in the same direction.

Evidence status:

Strong practitioner support; moderate academic support.

## Family B: Up-Volume / Down-Volume Breadth

This family weights participation by trading volume.

Common forms:

- Up-volume
- Down-volume
- Up-volume / down-volume ratio
- Arms Index / TRIN-style measures

Construct interpretation:

Participation is stronger when volume confirms price direction.

Evidence status:

Strong practitioner support; limited-to-moderate academic standardization.

## Family C: New High / New Low Breadth

This family measures how many securities are making fresh highs or lows.

Common forms:

- 52-week highs
- 52-week lows
- New highs minus new lows
- New high / new low ratio

Construct interpretation:

Broad market strength is higher when many securities confirm by making new highs.

Evidence status:

Strong practitioner support; moderate empirical relevance in market-internals literature.

## Family D: Moving-Average Breadth

This family measures the percentage of securities above or below a moving average.

Common forms:

- Percent above 50-day moving average
- Percent above 100-day moving average
- Percent above 200-day moving average
- Short-term versus long-term breadth spread

Construct interpretation:

Breadth is strong when many securities are in positive trend alignment.

Evidence status:

Strong practitioner support; conceptually connected to trend participation.

## Family E: Breadth Momentum / Breadth Thrust

This family measures rapid changes in participation.

Common forms:

- McClellan Oscillator-style breadth momentum
- Breadth thrust
- Acceleration in advancing participation
- Rapid improvement in percent-above-moving-average measures

Construct interpretation:

Fast broadening of participation may represent a regime shift in market internals.

Evidence status:

Mature practitioner tradition; empirical definitions vary substantially.

## Family F: Sector Breadth

This family measures whether participation is concentrated in a few sectors or distributed across many sectors.

Common forms:

- Number of sectors above moving average
- Sector advance / decline participation
- Sector equal-weight confirmation
- Sector leadership concentration

Construct interpretation:

Broader participation across sectors may indicate stronger market-wide confirmation.

Evidence status:

Conceptually strong; implementation depends heavily on sector classification quality.

## Family G: Equal-Weighted Versus Cap-Weighted Confirmation

This family compares average constituent behavior with capitalization-weighted index behavior.

Common forms:

- Equal-weighted index versus cap-weighted index
- Equal-weighted return confirmation
- Participation spread
- Concentration divergence

Construct interpretation:

Breadth is healthier when the average stock confirms the cap-weighted index.

Evidence status:

Strong conceptual relevance for modern concentrated equity indices.

## LR-001 Taxonomy Conclusion

The literature supports multiple plausible breadth families.

CD-001 must select one family and one exact operational definition before empirical validation begins.

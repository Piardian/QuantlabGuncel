# LR-001 Executive Summary

## Consensus-Level Findings

- The strongest academic evidence concerns medium-horizon **cross-sectional momentum**: ranking a fixed universe by prior returns and buying relative winners while selling relative losers, commonly over 3-12 month formation horizons.
- **Time-series momentum** is a distinct construct based on an asset's own return history; it has strong evidence in diversified futures studies but should not be conflated with stock cross-sectional momentum.
- Evidence extends beyond U.S. equities, but it is not uniform across every country, decade, size segment, or regime.
- Momentum has material implementation risks: turnover, costs, liquidity, capacity, and episodic crash risk.

## Relative Strength

In academic equity work, relative strength usually means cross-sectional prior-return ranking. Benchmark-relative return is a related but distinct operationalization. Industry-relative and risk-adjusted forms are also used.

## Production Comparison

The production rule is a long-only, fixed-threshold, SPY-relative, multi-horizon persistence gate. It resembles benchmark-relative multi-horizon momentum intuition, but does not match canonical ranked cross-sectional momentum, time-series momentum, sector-relative strength, or risk-adjusted practitioner index construction.

## Evidence Boundary

The literature supports no direct conclusion about `leadership_expansion_v1`. It identifies research families, known qualifications, and unresolved questions only.

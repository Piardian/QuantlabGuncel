# FUND-001 / LR-001

# Empirical Findings

## Descriptive Scope

This document summarizes empirical findings reported in the literature. It does not perform independent testing.

## Funding And Market Liquidity Interactions

The literature reports that funding constraints can reduce traders' and dealers' ability to provide market liquidity. During stress, this can produce reinforcing liquidity spirals.

Evidence classification: Strongly supported by theoretical and empirical crisis literature.

## Funding Stress During Crisis Periods

Funding stress measures commonly spike during major market disruptions such as the 2007-2009 financial crisis and March 2020.

Reported examples include:

- sharp widening of LIBOR-OIS and TED spreads in the global financial crisis,
- severe stress in institutional financial stress indexes during March 2020,
- increased demand for safety and liquidity during systemic stress.

Evidence classification: Strongly supported.

## LIBOR-OIS And TED Spread Evidence

LIBOR-OIS and TED spreads have been widely used as funding stress indicators. However, the literature also emphasizes that these spreads reflect both bank credit risk and liquidity risk.

Federal Reserve research further cautions that LIBOR became less correlated with actual bank funding costs as underlying unsecured funding markets changed.

Evidence classification: Moderately supported with important measurement caveats.

## Funding Stress Index Evidence

Institutional indexes such as the OFR Financial Stress Index treat funding as a distinct stress category. The Chicago Fed NFCI similarly incorporates many financial variables and includes risk, credit, and leverage components that can overlap with funding stress.

Evidence classification: Moderately supported.

## Dealer Balance-Sheet And Leverage Evidence

Research on intermediary balance sheets documents that leverage and balance-sheet size can vary procyclically. This supports the relevance of dealer funding capacity and balance-sheet constraints.

Evidence classification: Moderately supported.

## Interbank Funding Market Evidence

Research on the federal funds market during the financial crisis indicates that counterparty risk played an important role in interbank terms. This reinforces the need to separate funding stress from pure liquidity shortage.

Evidence classification: Moderately supported.

## Empirical Limitations

The empirical literature repeatedly shows that Funding Stress is observable, but not cleanly captured by a single proxy.

Common limitations:

- funding proxies often include credit risk,
- public repo and margin data may be limited,
- LIBOR discontinuation complicates historical continuity,
- composite indexes may mix constructs,
- central-bank intervention can change the meaning of observed funding variables.

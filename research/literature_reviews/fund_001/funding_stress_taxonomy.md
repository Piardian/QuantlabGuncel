# Funding Stress Taxonomy

## Purpose

This document summarizes major funding-stress taxonomies found in academic and practitioner literature.

No official construct is selected in LR-001.

## Taxonomy 1: Funding Liquidity

Funding liquidity refers to the ability of a market participant, bank, dealer, or institution to obtain cash or financing when needed.

Typical interpretation:

- ability to borrow,
- ability to roll short-term liabilities,
- ability to finance inventory,
- ability to meet margin calls,
- ability to avoid forced asset sales.

Funding stress occurs when this ability deteriorates.

## Taxonomy 2: Funding Liquidity Risk

Funding liquidity risk is the risk that funding liquidity deteriorates over a future horizon.

This is conceptually different from current funding liquidity. A market can appear liquid today while being vulnerable to future rollover or margin stress.

## Taxonomy 3: Interbank Funding Stress

This category focuses on stress in unsecured bank funding markets.

Common historical observables:

- LIBOR-OIS spread,
- TED spread,
- unsecured wholesale bank funding spreads,
- interbank borrowing conditions.

Limitation:

LIBOR-based measurement is less reliable after structural changes in bank funding markets and the LIBOR transition.

## Taxonomy 4: Secured Funding And Repo Stress

This category focuses on collateralized borrowing.

Common themes:

- repo rates,
- repo specialness,
- collateral scarcity,
- haircut increases,
- margin requirements,
- Treasury market funding pressure.

This is central to dealer financing and market-making capacity.

## Taxonomy 5: Collateral And Margin Stress

This category focuses on the feedback loop between falling asset prices, rising haircuts, margin calls, forced deleveraging, and declining market liquidity.

It is closely related to funding-market liquidity spirals.

## Taxonomy 6: Dealer Balance-Sheet Constraints

This category treats funding stress as a constraint on financial intermediaries.

Observed through:

- leverage,
- repo/reverse repo activity,
- balance-sheet contraction,
- dealer inventory constraints,
- intermediary risk-bearing capacity.

## Taxonomy 7: Composite Financial Stress Funding Components

Institutional indexes often include funding as one stress category.

Examples:

- OFR Financial Stress Index funding category,
- Chicago Fed NFCI components related to risk, credit, and leverage.

Strength:

- broad and institutionally recognized.

Weakness:

- may mix funding stress with credit, volatility, liquidity, and leverage.

## Taxonomy 8: Central-Bank Liquidity Operations

Some literature measures funding stress using central-bank operations, especially bidding behavior in open-market operations.

Strength:

- directly tied to demand for central-bank liquidity.

Weakness:

- may be jurisdiction-specific and less directly transferable to a US equity research platform.

## Taxonomy 9: Asset-Manager And Fund Liquidity Stress

This category focuses on liquidity mismatch and redemption pressure in funds.

Examples:

- money market funds,
- bond funds,
- open-end funds,
- redemption-driven selling.

This is related to funding stress but may require separate construct treatment.

## Summary

The literature does not define Funding Stress as one single observable. It is a family of related mechanisms centered on the cost, availability, and stability of financing.

CD-001 must narrow this taxonomy into one reproducible research construct.

# FUND-001 / LR-001

# Annotated Bibliography

## Brunnermeier and Pedersen, Market Liquidity and Funding Liquidity

Source: https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/22/6/2201/1592184

Contribution:

Defines a foundational mechanism linking funding liquidity and market liquidity. Shows how funding constraints, margins, and market liquidity can reinforce each other.

Use in FUND-001:

Core theoretical foundation.

## Drehmann and Nikolaou, Funding Liquidity Risk: Definition and Measurement

Sources:

- https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpwps/ecbwp1024.pdf
- https://www.bis.org/publ/work316.pdf

Contribution:

Provides explicit definitions of funding liquidity and funding liquidity risk, and proposes a measurement approach using central-bank open-market operations.

Use in FUND-001:

Important for CD-001 definitions and measurement discipline.

## OFR Financial Stress Index

Source: https://www.financialresearch.gov/financial-stress-index/

Contribution:

Institutional stress index that includes a funding category alongside credit, equity valuation, safe assets, and volatility.

Use in FUND-001:

Supports funding as an institutional stress dimension.

## OFR FSI Reference-Rate Transition Research

Source: https://www.financialresearch.gov/working-papers/files/OFRwp-23-07-transition-to-alternative-reference-rates-in-ofr-financial-stress-index.pdf

Contribution:

Documents implications of the transition from LIBOR-based inputs to alternative reference-rate inputs in financial stress measurement.

Use in FUND-001:

Important warning for post-LIBOR measurement.

## Chicago Fed National Financial Conditions Index

Source: https://www.chicagofed.org/research/data/nfci/about

Contribution:

Composite index of US financial conditions using a broad set of measures, with risk, credit, and leverage subcomponents.

Use in FUND-001:

Useful institutional reference, but not a pure funding-stress measure.

## SF Fed, What the LIBOR-OIS Spread Says

Source: https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2009/01/libor-financial-crisis/

Contribution:

Explains LIBOR-OIS as a crisis-period indicator containing credit and liquidity risk premia.

Use in FUND-001:

Useful historical proxy reference with explicit caveats.

## Federal Reserve, How Correlated is LIBOR with Bank Funding Costs?

Source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/how-correlated-is-libor-with-bank-funding-costs-20200629.html

Contribution:

Shows limitations of LIBOR as a proxy for bank funding costs, especially as funding markets changed.

Use in FUND-001:

Critical limitation for any LIBOR-based construct definition.

## Adrian and Shin, Liquidity and Leverage

Source: https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr328.pdf

Contribution:

Documents the role of intermediary balance sheets and leverage in financial conditions.

Use in FUND-001:

Supports dealer/intermediary funding-capacity interpretation.

## Afonso, Kovner, and Schoar, Stressed, Not Frozen

Source: https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr437.pdf

Contribution:

Studies the federal funds market during the financial crisis and emphasizes counterparty risk in interbank funding conditions.

Use in FUND-001:

Supports caution when interpreting interbank spread proxies.

## IMF Global Financial Stability Report, April 2020

Source: https://www.imf.org/en/publications/gfsr/issues/2020/04/14/global-financial-stability-report-april-2020

Contribution:

Documents abrupt tightening of global financial conditions and flight to safety/liquidity during the COVID-19 shock.

Use in FUND-001:

Contextual evidence for funding and liquidity stress during systemic shocks.

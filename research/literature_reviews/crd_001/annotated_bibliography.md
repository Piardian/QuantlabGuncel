# Annotated Bibliography

## Gilchrist and Zakrajsek, "Credit Spreads and Business Cycle Fluctuations"

This work is central to modern credit-stress research. It separates corporate bond spreads into expected-default and excess bond premium components and links credit-market stress to broader macro-financial conditions.

Evidence relevance: Strong.

## Federal Reserve FEDS Note, "Recession Risk and the Excess Bond Premium"

This Federal Reserve note applies excess bond premium concepts to recession-risk monitoring. It is useful for understanding institutional use of credit-premium measures.

Evidence relevance: Moderate to strong.

## FRED ICE BofA US High Yield Option-Adjusted Spread

This public series is a widely used high-yield credit spread proxy. It is useful for reproducible implementation candidates.

Evidence relevance: Strong as a data source.

## FRED Moody's Baa Corporate Bond Yield Relative to 10-Year Treasury

This long-history public series is useful for broad historical credit-stress measurement.

Evidence relevance: Strong as a data source.

## Duffie and Singleton, "Modeling Term Structures of Defaultable Bonds"

This paper is foundational for reduced-form modeling of defaultable debt and helps frame credit-risk compensation theoretically.

Evidence relevance: Strong theoretical foundation.

## Collin-Dufresne, Goldstein, and Martin, "The Determinants of Credit Spread Changes"

This paper documents that credit spread changes include a common systematic component not fully explained by standard structural determinants.

Evidence relevance: Strong.

## Longstaff, Mithal, and Neis, "Corporate Yield Spreads: Default Risk or Liquidity?"

This paper examines default and non-default components of corporate yield spreads and highlights time-varying liquidity-related components.

Evidence relevance: Strong.

## Dick-Nielsen, Feldhutter, and Lando, "Corporate Bond Liquidity Before and After the Onset of the Subprime Crisis"

This paper documents the role of corporate bond liquidity during crisis conditions.

Evidence relevance: Moderate to strong.

## OFR Financial Stress Index

The OFR index is an institutional composite stress measure that includes credit, equity valuation, funding, safe asset, and volatility categories.

Evidence relevance: Moderate as institutional context.

# CRD-001 / LR-001: Credit Stress Literature Review

## Study Identity

- Research program: Market Signal Discovery Program v3.0
- Construct ID: CRD-001
- Construct family: Credit Stress
- Stage: LR-001 Literature Review
- Stage objective: Describe what existing academic and professional literature supports regarding Credit Stress as a financial construct.

## Scope Boundary

This review does not define the final CRD-001 construct. It does not select variables, implement indicators, run experiments, evaluate predictive validity, or assess economic utility.

## Executive Finding

Credit Stress is strongly supported in the finance literature as a distinct and fundamental market construct. It is commonly represented through credit spreads, option-adjusted spreads, default-risk compensation, credit-risk premia, bond-market liquidity components, and broader financial stress indices.

The literature also shows that Credit Stress is not a single-dimensional object. Observed credit spreads may reflect expected default losses, risk premia, liquidity compensation, intermediary balance-sheet pressure, and macro-financial conditions. This means CD-001 must select one narrow and reproducible operational definition rather than treating all credit indicators as interchangeable.

## Research Questions

### 1. How is Credit Stress defined?

Credit Stress is typically defined as deterioration in debt-market conditions reflected by widening credit-risk compensation, impaired credit availability, rising default concern, or elevated required compensation for holding risky debt relative to safer instruments.

Common operational forms include:

- Corporate bond spreads over Treasury securities.
- Option-adjusted spreads for investment-grade or high-yield corporate debt.
- Rating-tier spreads such as Baa minus Treasury or Baa minus Aaa.
- CDS spreads or credit default swap indices.
- Excess bond premium measures that attempt to separate non-default spread components.
- Credit subcomponents inside financial stress indices.

### 2. Which theoretical mechanisms explain Credit Stress?

The literature supports several mechanisms:

- Default-risk repricing: investors demand greater compensation for expected default losses.
- Risk-premium repricing: spreads widen beyond expected default risk when risk appetite deteriorates.
- Liquidity stress: bond-market illiquidity can contribute to wider spreads, especially during crises.
- Financial accelerator and funding constraints: worsening credit conditions can amplify macro-financial weakness.
- Intermediary balance-sheet constraints: dealer and lender risk capacity can affect credit supply and spread behavior.
- Business-cycle linkage: credit spreads frequently rise around recessionary or stress periods.

### 3. Which taxonomies are commonly used?

The literature commonly separates Credit Stress into:

- Investment-grade credit stress.
- High-yield credit stress.
- Broad corporate spread stress.
- Default-risk versus non-default spread components.
- Bond-market liquidity stress.
- CDS-implied credit stress.
- Composite financial stress measures with credit-market inputs.

### 4. Which observable variables are commonly used?

Commonly used variables include:

- High-yield option-adjusted spread.
- Investment-grade option-adjusted spread.
- Moody's Baa yield minus Treasury yield.
- Moody's Baa minus Aaa spread.
- CDS index levels.
- Corporate default rates.
- Excess bond premium estimates.
- Credit components of financial stress indexes.

### 5. Which methodologies are most common?

Methodologies include:

- Simple spread-level monitoring.
- Spread changes and spread shocks.
- Normalized spread percentile or z-score states.
- Rating-bucket spread comparison.
- Structural credit-risk models.
- Reduced-form default-intensity models.
- Credit spread decomposition.
- Latent-state or regime approaches applied to credit variables.
- Composite stress-index construction.

### 6. What empirical evidence exists?

The empirical literature consistently treats credit spreads as important indicators of financial conditions. Gilchrist and Zakrajsek separate corporate spreads into default-risk and excess bond premium components and document that the excess bond premium is informative for economic activity. Other work finds that observed spread behavior includes common systematic variation not fully explained by standard credit-risk factors, and that liquidity components can become especially important during stress episodes.

This review summarizes those findings only. It does not independently test prediction or economic value.

### 7. What limitations are consistently reported?

Key limitations include:

- Credit spreads combine multiple mechanisms.
- Long-history series may be less precise than modern market-based measures.
- CDS and bond spreads may diverge due to liquidity and contract differences.
- High-yield spreads are stress-sensitive but have shorter and changing data histories.
- Financial stress indices are interpretable but can mix credit with liquidity, volatility, and funding conditions.
- Public data availability can constrain reproducibility.

## Evidence Classification

### Strongly Supported by Literature

- Credit spreads are a central observable representation of credit-market stress.
- Credit Stress is related to, but not identical with, volatility, liquidity, market regime, breadth, or correlation.
- Credit spread measures contain multiple components, including default and non-default premia.

### Moderately Supported

- High-yield credit spreads are especially sensitive to broad risk stress.
- Excess bond premium style decompositions can improve interpretation by separating expected default risk from broader risk appetite or intermediary-risk channels.

### Limited Evidence

- A single universally superior Credit Stress proxy exists for every research use case.
- Publicly reproducible data can fully recover proprietary or model-estimated credit stress measures.

### Conflicting Evidence

- The relative contribution of default risk, liquidity, and risk premia varies across period, market segment, and methodology.
- Bond-based and CDS-based credit stress measures may produce different interpretations during specific episodes.

## LR-001 Conclusion

Credit Stress should proceed to CD-001.

The literature provides sufficient scientific relevance, theoretical foundation, measurement maturity, and practical importance to justify a standalone construct program. The next stage must freeze one specific, reproducible operational definition and explicitly reject alternatives that are not selected.

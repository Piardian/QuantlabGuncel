# Research Gap Analysis

## Gap 1: Which Credit Stress Dimension Should CRD-001 Represent?

The literature supports multiple dimensions of credit stress. CD-001 must decide whether CRD-001 is broad credit stress, high-yield stress, investment-grade stress, default-risk stress, liquidity-adjusted stress, or excess premium stress.

## Gap 2: Public Reproducibility vs Theoretical Precision

Simple public spread series are reproducible but less theoretically pure. Decomposition measures may be more conceptually refined but harder to implement deterministically.

## Gap 3: Independence From Existing Constructs

Credit Stress is related to volatility, liquidity, market regime, breadth, and correlation. Future stages should evaluate whether the chosen CRD-001 implementation provides distinct information, but LR-001 does not test that.

## Gap 4: Crisis Sensitivity vs Normal-Period Stability

Some credit measures may be most informative during stress episodes and less variable in calm periods. Later validation must assess state stability and behavior across historical periods.

## Gap 5: Measurement Segment

Investment-grade and high-yield credit markets may represent different mechanisms. CD-001 must justify the selected segment.

## Gap 6: Modern Market Structure

Corporate bond market structure, ETF credit vehicles, and private credit growth may affect how credit stress appears in observable data.

# Theoretical Foundations

## Default-Risk Compensation

Credit spreads compensate investors for expected losses from default. Structural and reduced-form credit-risk models provide formal frameworks for linking debt values, default probabilities, recovery assumptions, and spread levels.

Evidence status: Strongly supported by literature.

## Risk Premium and Excess Bond Premium

Observed corporate credit spreads often move beyond what expected default losses alone imply. Excess bond premium research interprets part of the spread as compensation for bearing credit-market risk, changes in risk appetite, or intermediary balance-sheet constraints.

Evidence status: Strongly supported by literature.

## Liquidity Component

Corporate bonds can become difficult to trade during stress. Literature documents that illiquidity can contribute meaningfully to corporate spreads, especially during crisis periods.

Evidence status: Moderately supported, period-dependent.

## Financial Accelerator and Credit Supply

Credit conditions can amplify economic cycles. When financing conditions deteriorate, borrowing costs rise, balance sheets weaken, and economic activity may be constrained.

Evidence status: Strongly supported conceptually.

## Intermediary Constraints

Financial intermediaries and dealers affect credit supply and market liquidity. Constraints on their balance sheets can increase required compensation for holding credit risk.

Evidence status: Moderately supported.

## Business-Cycle Linkage

Credit stress often rises around recessions and market stress episodes. However, the direction, timing, and magnitude depend on measurement choice and market environment.

Evidence status: Strongly supported descriptively; predictive interpretation requires later stages.

# Annotated Bibliography

## Markowitz (1952), Portfolio Selection

Source: https://www.jstor.org/stable/2975974

Contribution: Establishes the mean-variance framework where covariance among assets is central to portfolio risk.

Relevance to COR-001: Strong theoretical foundation for treating correlation as a first-class financial construct.

Evidence classification: Strongly supported by literature.

## Bollerslev (1990), Modelling the Coherence in Short-Run Nominal Exchange Rates

Source: https://ideas.repec.org/a/tpr/restat/v72y1990i3p498-505.html

Contribution: Introduces a multivariate GARCH framework with time-varying variances and covariances under constant conditional correlations.

Relevance to COR-001: Important early conditional-covariance methodology.

Evidence classification: Strong methodological contribution.

## Engle (2002), Dynamic Conditional Correlation

Source: https://archive.nyu.edu/handle/2451/26482

Contribution: Introduces dynamic conditional correlation models for time-varying correlations.

Relevance to COR-001: Major methodology family for modeling changing correlations.

Evidence classification: Strong methodological contribution.

## Forbes and Rigobon (2002), No Contagion, Only Interdependence

Source: https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v57y2002i5p2223-2261.html

Contribution: Shows that heteroskedasticity can bias correlation-based contagion tests.

Relevance to COR-001: Critical methodological warning for interpreting stress-period correlation increases.

Evidence classification: Strongly supported methodological caution.

## Longin and Solnik (2001), Extreme Correlation of International Equity Markets

Source: https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v56y2001i2p649-676.html

Contribution: Studies extreme correlation and reports asymmetric dependence in international equity markets.

Relevance to COR-001: Supports considering tail dependence and downside-state correlation.

Evidence classification: Moderate to strong, context-dependent.

## Ledoit and Wolf (2003/2004), Improved Estimation of the Covariance Matrix

Source: https://www.ledoit.net/honey.pdf

Contribution: Develops shrinkage approaches to improve covariance-matrix estimation.

Relevance to COR-001: Important because correlation/covariance estimates can be noisy in high dimensions.

Evidence classification: Strong methodological contribution.

## RiskMetrics Technical Document

Source: https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/5915b101-4206-4ba0-aee2-3449d5c7e95a

Contribution: Practitioner framework for market risk measurement using volatility and correlation data.

Relevance to COR-001: Shows institutional adoption of correlation/covariance in risk systems.

Evidence classification: Practitioner evidence.

## Campbell, Lettau, Malkiel and Xu (2001), Have Individual Stocks Become More Volatile?

Source: https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v56y2001i1p1-43.html

Contribution: Decomposes stock volatility into market, industry and firm-level components and discusses changing explanatory power of market factors.

Relevance to COR-001: Useful for understanding market-wide versus idiosyncratic movement.

Evidence classification: Strong empirical contribution.

## Principal Components As A Measure Of Systemic Risk

Source: https://web.mit.edu/finlunch/Fall10/PCASystemicRisk.pdf

Contribution: Uses principal components and absorption ratio concepts to describe systemic risk concentration.

Relevance to COR-001: Supports the correlation-concentration branch of the construct landscape.

Evidence classification: Moderate, mechanism-relevant.

## NYU V-Lab Correlation Analysis

Source: https://vlab.stern.nyu.edu/docs/correlation

Contribution: Practitioner/academic resource describing correlation analysis and time variation.

Relevance to COR-001: Useful practical framing for changing correlations.

Evidence classification: Practitioner/academic reference.

## Cboe Implied Correlation Index

Source: https://www.cboe.com/us/indices/implied/

Contribution: Describes implied correlation as an option-market measure of expected index-component co-movement.

Relevance to COR-001: Supports implied-correlation branch.

Evidence classification: Practitioner methodology.

## ECB Financial Stability Review: Cross-Asset Correlations

Source: https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/financial-stability-publications/fsr/focus/2022/html/ecb.fsrbox202211_02~7abb48e333.en.html

Contribution: Discusses cross-asset correlation shifts and implications for portfolio allocation and concentration of risk.

Relevance to COR-001: Supports cross-asset correlation as a practical risk construct.

Evidence classification: Institutional evidence.

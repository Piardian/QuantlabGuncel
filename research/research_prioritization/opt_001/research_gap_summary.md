# OPT-001 / RP-001

# Research Gap Summary

## Gap 1: Construct Breadth

Options-implied information is too broad to define as one construct without further narrowing.

Potential meanings include expected volatility, variance risk premium, skew, tail risk, hedging demand, or informed trading.

## Gap 2: Data Availability

Index-level option-implied data may be accessible through public sources. Cross-sectional option surface data often requires paid datasets such as OptionMetrics or equivalent vendors.

## Gap 3: Measurement Complexity

Option surface research requires decisions about:

- moneyness,
- maturity,
- bid-ask filters,
- interpolation,
- American vs European exercise,
- dividends,
- liquidity filters,
- stale quotes,
- corporate actions.

These cannot be improvised after results are observed.

## Gap 4: Overlap With Existing Constructs

OPT-001 may overlap with:

- VOL-001 realized volatility,
- COR-001 realized correlation,
- MR-001 market regime,
- CRD-001 credit stress,
- LIQ-001 liquidity,
- FUND-001 funding spread stress.

Future studies must evaluate incremental identity carefully.

## Gap 5: Risk-Neutral vs Physical Probability

Options prices represent risk-neutral pricing, not direct real-world probability forecasts. Any predictive study must respect this distinction.

## Gap 6: Practitioner Measures vs Academic Measures

Practitioner measures such as put-call ratios or VIX term structure may be easy to compute but less theoretically precise than risk-neutral moments or variance risk premium.

LR-001 must distinguish evidence quality.

## Gap 7: Economic Use Case

Options-implied constructs may be useful for:

- risk monitoring,
- volatility forecasting,
- tail-risk detection,
- hedge timing,
- market stress context,
- option strategy research.

But none of these are established at RP-001.

# CRD-001 / RP-001 Research Prioritization

## Program

Market Signal Discovery Program v3.0

## Construct Candidate

CRD-001: Credit Stress Construct

## Stage Objective

Determine whether Credit Stress should proceed as a standalone scientific construct within the Market Signal Discovery Program.

This stage does not define the construct, evaluate predictive validity, or assess economic utility.

## Executive Decision

Final RP-001 decision: GO

Credit Stress is sufficiently recognized, theoretically grounded, measurable, and practically important to justify a dedicated research lifecycle.

## Supported By Evidence

Credit spreads are a central construct in corporate finance, macro-finance, monetary transmission, business-cycle analysis, financial stability, and risk management.

Credit stress is distinct from volatility, liquidity, breadth, market regime, and correlation because it measures compensation for credit risk, default risk, risk premia, and financing stress in debt markets.

Academic literature supports credit spreads and excess bond premia as important indicators of financial conditions and economic activity.

Institutional datasets such as FRED, financial stress indexes, and ICE BofA option-adjusted spreads provide reproducible measurement candidates for future stages.

## Not Supported At RP-001

No evidence is evaluated here that Credit Stress predicts returns.

No evidence is evaluated here that Credit Stress creates alpha.

No evidence is evaluated here that Credit Stress improves trading performance.

No specific credit spread, index, maturity, rating bucket, or threshold is selected in RP-001.

No implementation or production use is authorized.

## Research Priority Assessment

Overall priority: High

Rationale:

Credit stress directly addresses the cost and availability of corporate financing, default-risk compensation, risk premia, and debt-market stress. These dimensions are not fully represented by completed constructs such as MR-001, VOL-001, LIQ-001, BRD-001, or COR-001.

## Key Independence Argument

Credit stress is related to market volatility and liquidity stress, but it is not reducible to them.

Equity volatility can rise without a major credit event, and credit spreads can widen because of default-risk repricing, funding stress, or risk-premium shocks even when equity-market internals do not fully capture the change.

## Preliminary Literature Anchors

- Gilchrist and Zakrajsek show that credit spreads, especially the excess bond premium component, contain information about economic activity.
- FRED provides reproducible credit-spread series such as ICE BofA US High Yield OAS and Moody's Baa spread versus Treasury yield.
- Federal Reserve and OFR financial stress frameworks include credit spreads as a major stress category.
- Credit-spread decomposition literature distinguishes default risk, liquidity risk, taxes, recovery, jumps, and market-risk components.

## Recommended Next Stage

Proceed to CRD-001 / LR-001.

The literature review should focus on how credit stress is defined, measured, decomposed, validated, and interpreted across academic and practitioner literature.

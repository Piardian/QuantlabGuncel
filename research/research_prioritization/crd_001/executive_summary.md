Credit stress is scientifically important because it describes financing pressure, credit-risk repricing, default-risk compensation, and debt-market stress.

The strongest reason to proceed is that credit stress may capture a market-risk dimension not directly measured by existing constructs: stress in the cost and availability of credit.

The main caution is construct ambiguity. Credit spreads can reflect default risk, liquidity, risk premia, taxes, duration, recovery assumptions, and macro-financial conditions. CD-001 must therefore select one narrow operational definition after LR-001.

BACKGROUND

--------------------------------------------------

CRD-001 has completed RP-001 with a GO decision.

No construct definition has been selected yet.

No predictive or economic claims have been made.

--------------------------------------------------

PURPOSE

--------------------------------------------------

Conduct a comprehensive and unbiased literature review of Credit Stress as a financial construct.

The objective is to understand:

- how credit stress is defined
- how it is measured
- what theoretical mechanisms explain credit-spread changes
- where academic consensus exists
- where methodological disagreements remain
- which operational definitions are most suitable for later construct definition

This study must remain descriptive.

--------------------------------------------------

PRIMARY RESEARCH QUESTIONS

--------------------------------------------------

1.

How is Credit Stress defined in academic and practitioner literature?

2.

Which theoretical mechanisms explain credit-spread widening and credit stress?

3.

Which taxonomies are commonly used?

Examples may include:

- investment-grade spreads
- high-yield spreads
- Baa/Treasury spreads
- Baa/Aaa spreads
- option-adjusted spreads
- excess bond premium
- CDS-based measures
- financial stress index credit components

4.

Which observable variables are commonly used?

Examples may include:

- corporate bond yields
- Treasury yields
- option-adjusted spreads
- default rates
- CDS indexes
- financial stress indexes
- credit rating buckets

5.

Which methodologies are most frequently employed?

Examples:

- spread-level indicators
- spread-change indicators
- normalized spread states
- credit spread decomposition
- excess bond premium models
- financial conditions indexes
- rating-bucket spread comparisons

6.

What empirical evidence exists regarding credit stress behavior across recessions, crises, and monetary regimes?

Only summarize reported findings.

Do NOT independently evaluate predictive validity.

7.

What limitations are consistently reported?

8.

Which research gaps remain open?

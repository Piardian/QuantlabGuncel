# Construct Landscape

## What Market Liquidity Generally Means

Market Liquidity broadly refers to the ability to buy or sell an asset quickly, in meaningful size, at low cost, and without materially moving the price.

At RP-001, this is only a landscape description, not the frozen LIQ-001 definition.

## Common Liquidity Dimensions

The literature commonly discusses several dimensions:

- **Tightness:** trading cost or bid-ask spread.
- **Depth:** available quantity near current prices.
- **Immediacy:** speed of execution.
- **Breadth:** availability of liquidity across sizes and price levels.
- **Resiliency:** speed with which liquidity recovers after shocks.
- **Price impact:** how much prices move in response to trading.

## Related But Distinct Constructs

| Construct | Relationship to liquidity | Distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Volatility | Often rises when liquidity deteriorates. | Volatility measures price variability; liquidity measures trading friction and market capacity. |
| Market Regime | Stress regimes often include poor liquidity. | Regime is a broad latent state; liquidity is a specific market-quality dimension. |
| Momentum | Can be affected by liquidity and transaction costs. | Momentum measures return persistence; liquidity measures tradability. |
| Volume | Often used as a proxy for liquidity. | Volume alone does not measure trading cost or price impact. |
| Spread | A direct liquidity measure. | Spread is one dimension, not the full construct. |

## Construct Independence Assessment

Market Liquidity is sufficiently independent to justify its own research lifecycle.

It overlaps with market regime during stress but has a different theoretical object: trading friction, not latent market state.

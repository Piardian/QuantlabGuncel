# Literature Overview

## High-Level Finding

Market Liquidity has a mature and substantial literature across market microstructure, asset pricing, and financial stability.

## Key Literature Themes

### Market Microstructure

Microstructure research studies how trading mechanisms, order flow, bid-ask spreads, market depth, and price impact determine trading costs and market quality. Liquidity is one of its central constructs.

### Liquidity Dimensions

Foundational liquidity discussions emphasize that liquidity is multidimensional. Common dimensions include tightness, depth, immediacy, breadth, and resiliency. The IMF working paper *Measuring Liquidity in Financial Markets* describes these broad dimensions, and later surveys continue to emphasize this multidimensional nature.

### Illiquidity and Expected Returns

Amihud (2002) studies illiquidity using a daily return-to-dollar-volume proxy and reports a relationship between expected market illiquidity and expected excess returns. This supports liquidity as a measurable risk or cost-related construct, though RP-001 does not evaluate return prediction.

### Systematic Liquidity Risk

Pastor and Stambaugh (2003) investigate whether market-wide liquidity is a priced state variable. Acharya and Pedersen (2005) develop a liquidity-adjusted CAPM framework in which expected returns depend on expected liquidity and liquidity-related covariances.

### Aggregate Market Liquidity

Chordia, Roll, and Subrahmanyam (2001) study aggregate market spreads, depths, and trading activity, supporting the idea that liquidity can be studied at market-wide rather than only single-security level.

### Professional and Policy Relevance

Central banks and market-structure practitioners treat liquidity as important for market stability, execution quality, and financial stress monitoring. This supports the practical relevance of the construct.

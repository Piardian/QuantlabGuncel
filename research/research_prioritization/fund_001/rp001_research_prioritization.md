## Rationale

Funding Stress is recognized in academic and institutional finance as a distinct market construct. It describes stress in the ability of financial institutions, traders, or market participants to obtain funding, roll liabilities, borrow against collateral, or maintain leveraged positions under changing margins and risk conditions.

The literature distinguishes funding liquidity from market liquidity, while also showing that the two can reinforce one another during stress. Institutional financial stress frameworks also include funding-related indicators as a separate category or subcomponent.

## Construct Independence

Funding Stress is related to but not identical with:

- Market liquidity: ease of trading assets.
- Credit stress: compensation for bearing credit/default risk.
- Volatility state: magnitude of asset price variation.
- Correlation state: co-movement and common-factor dominance.
- Market regime: broad latent market environment.

Funding Stress focuses on the availability and cost of financing positions, collateral, and balance-sheet capacity.

## Data Availability Considerations

Potential future measurement candidates include:

- LIBOR-OIS or successor reference-rate spreads.
- TED spread or Treasury-Eurodollar style measures.
- Repo and secured funding indicators.
- SOFR-related spreads.
- Financial stress index funding subcomponents.
- NFCI risk or leverage subcomponents.
- Dealer/intermediary balance-sheet proxies.

RP-001 does not select any of these.

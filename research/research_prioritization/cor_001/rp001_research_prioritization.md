# COR-001 / RP-001 Research Prioritization

## Program

Market Signal Discovery Program v3.0

## Construct Candidate

COR-001: Market Correlation / Correlation State Construct

## Stage Objective

Determine whether Market Correlation should proceed as a standalone scientific construct within the Market Signal Discovery Program.

This stage does not define the construct, evaluate predictive validity, or assess economic utility.

## Executive Decision

Final RP-001 decision: GO

Market Correlation is sufficiently recognized, theoretically grounded, measurable, and practically important to justify a dedicated research lifecycle.

## Supported By Evidence

Market correlation is a fundamental concept in portfolio theory, diversification, systemic risk, contagion/interdependence research, and risk management.

Correlation is distinct from volatility, liquidity, breadth, and market regime because it measures co-movement and dependence structure rather than the level of risk, market participation, tradeability, or latent state classification.

Empirical and practitioner literature repeatedly notes that correlations are time-varying and often rise during market stress, reducing diversification benefits.

Correlation measurement has known methodological complications, especially under heteroskedasticity, high volatility, short samples, nonlinear dependence, and changing market structure.

## Not Supported At RP-001

No evidence is evaluated here that Market Correlation predicts returns.

No evidence is evaluated here that Market Correlation creates alpha.

No evidence is evaluated here that Market Correlation improves trading performance.

No specific correlation estimator is selected in RP-001.

No specific universe, window, or threshold is frozen in RP-001.

## Research Priority Assessment

Overall priority: High

Rationale:

Market correlation directly addresses portfolio diversification, common risk exposure, systemic risk, and stress propagation. These dimensions are not fully represented by completed constructs such as MR-001, VOL-001, LIQ-001, or BRD-001.

## Key Independence Argument

Correlation is related to volatility but not reducible to volatility.

A market can be volatile with low cross-asset co-movement, or moderately volatile with high co-movement. The second case is especially important for portfolio construction because diversification can fail even when individual volatility measures do not fully capture concentration of common movement.

## Preliminary Literature Anchors

- Forbes and Rigobon, "No Contagion, Only Interdependence" highlights that correlation-based contagion analysis is affected by heteroskedasticity and requires methodological care.
- NYU V-Lab describes correlations as time-varying and often elevated during market stress.
- ECB financial stability research discusses how cross-asset correlation changes affect portfolio allocation and concentration of risk.
- Empirical research on stock correlations under market stress documents state dependence in equity correlations.
